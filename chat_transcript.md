## Project Purpose

This workspace is for researching and developing guidance related to academic funding proposals, with a particular focus on the UKRI NERC Pushing the Frontiers call.

Work in this project should help produce:

- high-quality research notes
- clear proposal-writing guidance
- call-specific interpretation

## Core Working Rules

1. Do not start substantive research unless the user has asked for it.
2. Prioritize accuracy over speed, especially for funder rules, eligibility, dates, and assessment criteria.
3. Treat UKRI and NERC materials as the primary authority for call-specific claims.
4. Clearly separate:
   - source-backed facts
   - interpretation
   - recommendations
5. Do not invent requirements, reviewer criteria, or deadlines that are not supported by sources.
6. When information may have changed recently, verify it before presenting it as current.
7. Use concise, practical language aimed at helping draft or improve a real proposal.

## Research Priorities

When research begins, use this order of priority:

1. Official UKRI and NERC call pages
2. Official application guidance and assessment criteria
3. Official funding service or scheme documents
4. Reputable university research office guidance
5. Trusted academic proposal-writing resources

Avoid relying on:

- low-authority blogs
- unattributed summaries
- AI-generated secondary material
- forum posts unless explicitly needed for context

## Project-Specific Expectations

For this project, all outputs should address both:

1. What makes a good academic funding proposal in general
2. What matters specifically for the UKRI NERC Pushing the Frontiers call

Future work should explicitly compare those two levels rather than blending them together.

## Source Handling

- Record links for all important sources used.
- Prefer primary sources for any claim about scheme rules or assessment.
- If a source is ambiguous, say so plainly.
- If guidance conflicts across sources, flag the conflict and explain which source is more authoritative.
- Use exact dates when discussing deadlines, rounds, or “current” call details.

## Writing Standards

All project documents should be:

- written in Markdown
- structured for fast scanning
- useful to a researcher preparing a live application
- free of filler and generic grant-writing clichés

Preferred output structure:

1. Short executive summary
2. General principles
3. Call-specific analysis
4. Comparison or gap analysis
5. Practical checklist or next actions

## Citation Standards

- Include source links in research outputs.
- Attribute funder-specific claims to official sources wherever possible.
- Keep quotation minimal; paraphrase unless exact wording is necessary.
- When making an inference, label it clearly as an inference.

## File Conventions

- Keep top-level files few and purposeful.
- Use descriptive Markdown filenames in lowercase with hyphens.
- Prefer updating existing planning or research documents over creating duplicates.
- Create new files only when they represent a distinct deliverable.

## Out of Scope

Unless the user asks otherwise, do not:

- draft fictional proposal content
- fabricate project data, partnerships, or preliminary results
- assume eligibility without checking
- optimize for persuasive tone at the expense of accuracy

## Default Deliverable Style

The default deliverable for this workspace should be a practical briefing document that helps the user:

- understand the call
- understand what strong proposals do well
- identify likely reviewer expectations
- turn the research into a stronger draft

## Proposal Draft Review Rules

When reviewing a proposal draft in this project:

1. Prioritize substantive weaknesses over sentence-level polishing.
2. Review through both lenses:
   - general proposal quality
   - fit to the specific UKRI NERC call
3. Focus first on high-stakes issues such as:
   - unclear research question
   - weak novelty or frontier case
   - mismatch between aims and methods
   - weak fit to call scope
   - unsupported feasibility
   - vague impact or outcomes
   - poor justification of team, resources, or timing
4. Distinguish clearly between:
   - major strategic issues
   - moderate structural issues
   - minor wording edits
5. Do not let stylistic edits obscure problems with logic, evidence, or funder fit.
6. When possible, explain comments in reviewer-style terms:
   - what concern a reviewer may have
   - why it matters
   - what would strengthen the section
7. Be direct but constructive. The goal is to improve fundability, not just readability.
8. If a section appears strong, say why, so the user knows what to preserve.

Preferred draft-review output order:

1. Overall assessment
2. Major issues
3. Section-by-section comments
4. Priority revisions
5. Optional line edits
