# Scoring Rubric: UKRI NERC Pushing the Frontiers

Research date: 4 May 2026

## Purpose

This rubric is designed to help assess a project idea or draft proposal against both:

1. general features of strong academic funding proposals
2. the specific expectations of the current UKRI NERC `Pushing the frontiers of environmental research` opportunity

It is a practical working tool, not an official UKRI scoring sheet.

## How to Use This Rubric

- Score each section from `1` to `6`
- Use the guidance below to decide the score
- Add short notes on strengths, weaknesses, and priority revisions
- Focus first on major weaknesses that would damage fundability

Suggested interpretation:

- `6` = exceptional
- `5` = excellent
- `4` = very good
- `3` = good but with clear weaknesses
- `2` = weak and not currently competitive
- `1` = poor or fundamentally flawed

## Quick Outcome Guide

- Mostly `5-6`: potentially competitive for this scheme
- Mostly `4`: promising, but likely needs sharpening to stand out
- Any `2` in a core section: high risk of not being competitive
- Any `1`: major redesign needed

## Section 1: Vision and Frontier Quality

### What to Assess

- Is the research question clear, important, and ambitious?
- Does the project genuinely push beyond the current state of knowledge?
- Is the frontier claim specific rather than rhetorical?
- Is the work timely?
- Is the potential gain large enough to justify this scheme?

### Score Anchors

`6`:

- The project is intellectually striking, clearly important, and convincingly frontier-pushing.
- The novelty is precise and compelling.
- The proposal makes a strong case for why this matters now.

`5`:

- The project is clearly ambitious and important.
- The originality case is strong, though not quite outstanding in every respect.

`4`:

- The project is worthwhile and somewhat novel, but the frontier claim is not fully sharp.
- The importance is good but not maximally compelling.

`3`:

- The core idea is sound, but the novelty or importance case is only moderate.
- The proposal risks reading as incremental.

`2`:

- The project does not convincingly read as frontier research.
- The significance or originality case is weak or vague.

`1`:

- The proposal lacks a clear, important, or fundable vision.

### Reviewer Questions

- What changes if this project succeeds?
- Is this truly adventurous, or just described that way?
- Why should this be funded through this scheme rather than a routine grant?

## Section 2: Research Question and Rationale

### What to Assess

- Is the central question clearly defined?
- Is the scientific gap explicit?
- Is the rationale logically developed from the current state of knowledge?
- Is the “why now” case convincing?

### Score Anchors

`6`:

- The research question is exceptionally clear and well motivated.
- The knowledge gap is explicit and significant.
- The rationale is tight and persuasive.

`5`:

- The question and rationale are strong and clear.
- The gap and timing are well explained.

`4`:

- The question is clear, but parts of the rationale or gap framing need tightening.

`3`:

- The question is understandable, but the rationale is underdeveloped or partially generic.

`2`:

- The core question or gap is hard to pin down.
- The project motivation feels weak or assumed.

`1`:

- The proposal does not establish a coherent research question or rationale.

## Section 3: Approach and Methodological Strength

### What to Assess

- Is the approach well matched to the question?
- Is the methodology clear and credible?
- Does the plan show how objectives will actually be achieved?
- Is prior work built on intelligently where relevant?
- Does the project design fit the level of ambition?

### Score Anchors

`6`:

- The approach is rigorous, well aligned, and highly convincing.
- The methodology is clear, appropriate, and fully supports the aims.

`5`:

- The design is strong and credible with only minor weaknesses.

`4`:

- The approach is generally solid, but some parts lack precision or strength.

`3`:

- The approach is plausible, but important elements remain unclear, thin, or not fully justified.

`2`:

- The methodology is weak, mismatched, or underdeveloped.

`1`:

- The approach is not credible as a route to answering the research question.

### Reviewer Questions

- If I accept the question, do I believe this design can answer it?
- Are there any obvious missing steps or unsupported leaps?

## Section 4: Feasibility, Risk, and Delivery

### What to Assess

- Is the project feasible within the proposed time and structure?
- Are major risks identified and handled intelligently?
- Is there a realistic workplan with milestones and sequencing?
- Does the proposal balance ambition with credible delivery?

### Score Anchors

`6`:

- The proposal is ambitious but clearly deliverable.
- Risks are well identified, proportionately managed, and integrated into the design.
- The workplan is highly credible.

`5`:

- Feasibility is strong and risks are well handled.
- Delivery planning is convincing.

`4`:

- The project looks feasible, but some delivery or risk-management points need tightening.

`3`:

- Delivery appears possible, but confidence is weakened by vague planning or thin risk treatment.

`2`:

- Feasibility is doubtful.
- Risks are underplayed or poorly managed.

`1`:

- The proposal is not credibly deliverable as written.

### Reviewer Questions

- Is the project plan real, or just optimistic?
- Has the team shown they understand the scientific and practical risks?

## Section 5: Team Capability and Research Environment

### What to Assess

- Does the team have the right expertise mix?
- Is the capability case tied directly to the proposed work?
- Is leadership and management credible?
- Does the proposal show access to the right environment, collaborations, facilities, and infrastructure?
- Is the case made appropriately for career stage rather than prestige alone?

### Score Anchors

`6`:

- The team is exceptionally well matched to the project.
- The capability case is precise, convincing, and fully aligned to delivery.
- The environment is a clear strength.

`5`:

- The team and environment are strong and clearly support the work.

`4`:

- The team looks capable, but some roles, expertise, or support structures need clearer explanation.

`3`:

- The team is plausible, but the case is generic, incomplete, or uneven.

`2`:

- There are visible gaps in expertise, leadership, environment, or access.

`1`:

- The proposal does not show that the team can realistically deliver the work.

### Reviewer Questions

- Why is this the right team for this project?
- Is capability evidenced, or merely asserted?

## Section 6: Fit to NERC and Scheme Purpose

### What to Assess

- Is the project predominantly within NERC remit?
- Is the proposal aligned with the scheme’s curiosity-driven, adventurous, high-reward purpose?
- Does it avoid being too incremental, too routine, or too loosely connected to environmental science?
- If interdisciplinary, is the NERC fit still clear?

### Score Anchors

`6`:

- NERC remit fit and scheme fit are exceptionally clear.
- The project feels exactly suited to this opportunity.

`5`:

- Fit is strong and convincing.

`4`:

- Fit is probably sound, but some aspects would benefit from clearer framing.

`3`:

- Fit is arguable but not fully secured in the writing.

`2`:

- Remit or scheme fit is uncertain.

`1`:

- The project is poorly matched to the opportunity.

### Reviewer Questions

- Why should NERC fund this?
- Why should this be funded through Pushing the Frontiers specifically?

## Section 7: Ethics, Responsible Research, and Wider Implications

### What to Assess

- Have ethical and RRI issues been properly identified?
- Does the proposal address societal, environmental, and research-integrity implications where relevant?
- Are data, fieldwork, human, animal, dual-use, or environmental issues managed well?
- Is responsible research embedded in the design rather than treated as a formality?

### Score Anchors

`6`:

- Ethical and RRI issues are handled thoughtfully, specifically, and proportionately.
- The proposal shows mature awareness of wider implications and mitigation.

`5`:

- Ethical and RRI treatment is strong and credible.

`4`:

- The section is adequate, but could be more specific or better integrated.

`3`:

- Basic issues are acknowledged, but treatment is limited or somewhat generic.

`2`:

- Important issues are insufficiently addressed.

`1`:

- The proposal neglects serious ethical or RRI considerations.

## Section 8: Resources and Cost Justification

### What to Assess

- Are requested resources appropriate to the science?
- Is the budget proportionate and justified?
- Are expensive items clearly necessary?
- Does the project avoid both under-resourcing and bloat?
- Are facilities, equipment, travel, and specialist support explained credibly?

### Score Anchors

`6`:

- Resources are exceptionally well matched, strategic, and convincingly justified.

`5`:

- The resource case is strong and appropriate with only minor questions.

`4`:

- The budget looks broadly reasonable, but some items need clearer justification.

`3`:

- The resource case is mixed, with visible gaps, weak justifications, or some inefficiency.

`2`:

- Important costs are unclear, unsupported, or poorly aligned with the science.

`1`:

- The resource plan is not credible.

### Reviewer Questions

- Is this the minimum credible cost of doing the project well?
- Do the requested resources obviously serve the research aims?

## Section 9: Clarity, Structure, and Readability

### What to Assess

- Is the proposal easy to follow?
- Is the logic visible from question to method to outcomes?
- Is it written for assessors rather than only for close specialists?
- Are sections well structured and proportioned?
- Are visuals, if used, actually helpful?

### Score Anchors

`6`:

- The proposal is exceptionally clear, coherent, and easy to assess.
- The writing strengthens the scientific case.

`5`:

- The proposal is very clear and well structured.

`4`:

- The proposal is readable, but some sections could be tighter or better signposted.

`3`:

- The science is there, but the presentation weakens its impact.

`2`:

- Clarity problems make it difficult to judge the proposal fairly.

`1`:

- The proposal is confusing, poorly structured, or hard to assess.

## Overall Judgment

### Suggested Overall Categories

- `Fundable shape`: strong candidate for further development or submission
- `Promising but not yet competitive`: good core idea, but important revisions needed
- `Borderline`: multiple weaknesses likely to concern reviewers
- `Not competitive in current form`: substantial redesign needed

### Final Decision Prompts

- What are the top three strengths that should be preserved?
- What are the top three weaknesses most likely to damage reviewer confidence?
- What is the single biggest revision that would improve competitiveness?
- Does the proposal currently feel more ambitious than credible, more credible than ambitious, or well balanced?

## High-Priority Failure Flags

If any of the following are true, the proposal is unlikely to be competitive without major revision:

- the frontier claim is vague or unconvincing
- the research question is not sharply defined
- the method does not convincingly answer the question
- the project is too ambitious for the delivery plan
- NERC remit fit is unclear
- the team capability case is generic or incomplete
- expensive resources are poorly justified
- the proposal is difficult to follow
